Fruit Tree Orchards

Orchards with fruit trees can often be confused with cacao systems, especially where both occur in mosaic landscapes. Fruit trees typically have taller, broader canopies than cacao and are spaced more widely, often around 7–10 meters apart, compared to cacao’s denser planting pattern of 2–4 meters. This difference in spacing produces a more open and regularly patterned arrangement in high-resolution imagery. The canopy texture of fruit trees is usually smoother and more continuous than the finer, multi-layered canopy typical of cacao agroforestry systems, where shade trees and cacao plants create mixed vertical structures.

This section highlights common orchard crops such as mango, citrus, jackfruit, avocado, and breadfruit, which may resemble cacao from above. While their appearance varies by species and management, interpreters can use cues such as canopy size, crown shape, spatial regularity, and surrounding land-use patterns to distinguish fruit orchards from cacao plantations.
