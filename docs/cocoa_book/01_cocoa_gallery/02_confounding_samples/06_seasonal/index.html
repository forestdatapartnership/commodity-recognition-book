Seasonal Crops

In cacao-growing regions, fallow lands with dense secondary vegetation are frequent sources of confusion during image interpretation. When vegetation regrows naturally after cropping, the resulting mix of shrubs, young trees, and herbaceous plants can create a canopy that appears similar to cacao plantations in high-resolution imagery. However, these areas usually lack the organized planting structure and consistent spacing characteristic of cacao systems. Over time, time-series analysis can reveal the difference: fallow vegetation changes rapidly in cover and composition, while cacao plantations maintain a stable canopy across years.

Seasonal or annual agriculture in cacao landscapes – such as cassava, maize, rice, or sugarcane – can also create visual confusion when seen during late growth stages, especially in mixed or intercropped systems. These crops typically exhibit short-lived, uniform canopies that change dramatically between growing and harvesting seasons. Their strong phenological cycles (rapid growth, harvest, and bare-soil phases) contrast sharply with the evergreen and perennial nature of cacao.

Description: Some perennial crops are grown on an annual basis, in this case cassava. The planting pattern can be very similar to cocoa, however the rows are closer together and the canopy is more homogenous. The use of time series can show the frequent harvest of the crop.
