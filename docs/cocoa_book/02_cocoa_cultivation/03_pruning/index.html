Pruning Practices

Overview

Pruning is a key management practice in cocoa cultivation, essential for maintaining healthy trees and improving yields. It involves removing dead, diseased, or excess branches to allow more light into the canopy and to support balanced growth. In well-managed farms, pruning is typically carried out twice a year.

There are two main types of pruning:

  • Phytosanitary pruning removes dead or diseased branches to reduce pest and disease pressure.
  • Structural pruning shapes the tree to improve light penetration and air circulation, enhancing productivity and ease of harvest.

While most farmers prune to some extent, the quality of pruning can vary. In smallholder systems, pruning may be limited to removing lower branches, leaving the canopy dense; this type of pruning does not impact the appearance of cocoa trees in high-resolution imagery. In contrast, large plantations – such as those in Brazil or Ecuador – often contract out pruning tasks, resulting in more consistent and systematic canopy management.

These differences in pruning practices can affect how cocoa plots appear in high-resolution imagery, especially in terms of canopy uniformity.
