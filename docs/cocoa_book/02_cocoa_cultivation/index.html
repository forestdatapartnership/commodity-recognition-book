Understanding Cocoa Cultivation

To recognize cocoa in satellite imagery, it helps to understand how it’s grown and managed.

Cocoa is grown across the humid tropics by an estimated 5–6 million farmers, the majority of whom are smallholders. While cocoa is cultivated in over 60 countries (FAO, 2021), production is highly concentrated: just seven countries account for nearly 90% of global output. West Africa dominates, with Côte d’Ivoire and Ghana alone producing over 60% of the world’s cocoa in the 2020/21 season (ICCO, 2021). Smaller but significant production also takes place in Latin America and South and Southeast Asia.

This section explores the key biological traits and farming practices that shape how cocoa fields appear from above. From canopy structure to shade levels, pruning styles to intercropping, each element leaves visual traces that influence how cocoa is interpreted in high-resolution imagery.

You’ll learn about:

  • The different management systems used to grow cocoa around the world
  • How planting styles, intercropping, and pruning affect field layout and texture
  • What cocoa looks like at different stages of growth, and how appearance varies by variety and age

This background will help you interpret cocoa landscapes more accurately, avoid confusion with similar crops, and better understand the diversity shown in the Cocoa Gallery.
