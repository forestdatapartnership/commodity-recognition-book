Coffee Varieties

Overview

Arabica and Robusta are the two main varieties of coffee, each with distinct visual and geographic characteristics.

Arabica, originating in Ethiopia, thrives at higher elevations between 700–2200 meters above sea level (masl) and in cooler temperatures of 16–24°C. It is less resistant to pests and diseases, with a natural tree height of 5–8 meters. Arabica beans have lower caffeine content and are known for their delicate flavors, accounting for about 60% of global coffee production, with an annual output of approximately 96 million bags.

Robusta, on the other hand, originates from West and Central Africa and grows well at lower elevations of 0–900 masl (up to 1600 masl in some cases) and in warmer temperatures of 21–30°C. It is highly resistant to pests and diseases, with taller trees reaching 8–15 meters. Robusta beans contain higher caffeine levels, contributing to their stronger and more bitter flavor profile. This variety represents about 40% of global coffee production, with an annual output of 73 million bags.

Distinguishing between Robusta and Arabica coffee systems in high-resolution imagery can be challenging due to their similarities. However, subtle differences can provide clues: Robusta systems typically feature larger trees with a denser, light-green canopy, while Arabica systems often have smaller trees with a more open canopy and a darker green. Different regions favor each variety; for example, Robusta is common in Vietnam, while Arabica is prevalent in Colombia.
