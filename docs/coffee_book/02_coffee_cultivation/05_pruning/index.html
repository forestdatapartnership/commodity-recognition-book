Pruning Practices

Overview

Pruning is a common practice in coffee cultivation, typically performed annually after the harvest to maintain tree structure and optimize productivity, though its intensity and frequency vary across regions. In Colombia, for example, coffee trees are pruned frequently and kept short, maintaining a bush-like shape at around 1.5 meters in height. Conversely, in Vietnam, coffee trees are less frequently pruned and can grow taller, often exceeding two meters. These differences are evident in high-resolution imagery: heavily pruned coffee plantations display a more defined row pattern with visible soil between trees, whereas less frequently pruned systems exhibit a denser canopy, with larger, rounder, and “fluffier” tree crowns.
