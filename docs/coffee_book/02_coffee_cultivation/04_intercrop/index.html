Intercropping Practices

Overview

Beyond agroforestry practices, coffee cultivation often incorporates a wide range of intercropping solutions that, while not providing shade, offer additional benefits such as supplementary income or enhanced soil management. In terms of ground cover, many systems practice weeding, leaving bare soil visible between coffee plants. However, in some cases, no weeding is performed, resulting in low vegetation (often grass) covering the soil between the trees, impacting the color in high-resolution imagery. Certain systems utilize intercropped cover crops, such as beans or maize, or cultivars that contribute to soil health and nitrogen fixation. Additionally, vine crops like black pepper are sometimes grown on poles arranged in rows between the coffee plants. Intercropped coffee systems tend to have a dense canopy with little to no visible soil in high-resolution imagery.
