Understanding Coffee Cultivation

To recognize coffee in satellite imagery, it helps to understand how it’s grown. This section explores the key agricultural practices and biological features that shape how coffee fields look from above. From shade trees to pruning cycles, from intercropping to irrigation, each factor leaves visual clues in the landscape.

You’ll learn about:

  • The different systems used to grow coffee around the world
  • How farm management influences field structure and canopy appearance
  • What to expect across the coffee plant’s life cycle

This background will help you interpret images more confidently, distinguish coffee from other crops, and better understand the variation seen in the Coffee Gallery.

Shade Management

The level of shade in coffee systems significantly influences how they appear in high-resolution imagery.

Intercropping Practices

In many coffee systems, intercropping is used to enhance income or improve soil management; these practices result in denser canopy structures and often obscure soil visibility in high-resolution imagery.

Pruning Practices

Pruning affects the height, density, and overall structure of coffee trees, impacting the way they appear in satellite imagery.

Coffee Varieties

The appearance of coffee systems in imagery can vary significantly due to regional differences in coffee varieties and their associated growing conditions.

Irrigation Practices

Irrigation is a key feature of highly intensified coffee systems, particularly in regions like Vietnam. Plots with irrigation systems often exhibit uniform growth and phenology, resulting in distinct patterns visible in high-resolution imagery.

Seasonal Cycles

Robusta coffee goes through a growth cycle of two years, throughout which its appearance varies in satellite imagery.
