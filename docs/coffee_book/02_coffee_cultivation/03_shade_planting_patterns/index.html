Shade Management

Overview

Coffee systems can be classified into five primary types based on their vertical and compositional diversity of shade:

  • Understory rustic polyculture
  • Traditional polyculture
  • Commercial polyculture
  • Shaded monoculture
  • Full sun monoculture.

These systems vary significantly in the amount of shade they provide, which can be broadly categorized as high, medium, low, or no shade. Rustic polyculture systems maintain an almost intact forest canopy, with minimal modifications to the understory, allowing coffee plants to grow in a natural, forest-like environment. These systems are typically classified as highly shaded and, from high-resolution imagery, closely resemble forests with a seemingly random distribution of large and small trees. This makes them particularly difficult to distinguish visually.

In contrast, traditional polyculture retains the forest canopy but replaces the understory with coffee plants. These systems provide a range of high to medium levels of shade and are also challenging to identify visually. However, the structured arrangement of coffee plants beneath the larger canopy trees can sometimes be discerned. Commercial polyculture takes a more intensive approach, removing both the natural canopy and understory and replacing them with coffee plants and smaller shade trees chosen for their commercial value. These systems generally provide medium to low levels of shade and are characterized by clear, structured patterns, often with orderly rows of coffee plants and shade trees. Shaded monoculture, a modernized system, eliminates the natural vegetation entirely, introducing leguminous trees specifically planted to create a shade canopy for coffee crops. Similar to commercial polyculture, these systems offer medium to low levels of shade and display highly organized layouts, often with alternating rows of shade trees and coffee plants. Finally, full sun monoculture removes all natural vegetation, replacing it entirely with coffee plants. This system provides no shade at all, resulting in an open and highly uniform planting structure.
