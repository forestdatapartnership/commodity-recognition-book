Coffee Cultivation Systems

Overview

Coffee systems range from highly shaded, rustic polycultures with dense, forest-like canopies to open, sun-exposed monocultures. These systems reflect different management approaches, from preserving natural vegetation to maximizing productivity in structured rows.

In most coffee plantations, trees are typically spaced 2.5 to 3 meters apart, arranged in continuous rows with sufficient gaps to allow workers to move and harvest the coffee beans by hand. In regions where mechanized harvesting is common, like Brazil, plantations often adopt highly structured layouts, with clearly defined rows or even circular patterns to accommodate machinery. Conversely, in East Africa, coffee is frequently cultivated in small, garden-like plots where trees are scattered and intercropped with other food crops.
