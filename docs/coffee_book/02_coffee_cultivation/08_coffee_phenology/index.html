Seasonal Cycles

Coffee phenology

Robusta coffee goes through a growth cycle of two years that can impact how it looks in satellite imagery. Understanding this cycle, called phenology, can help you interpret changes in canopy color, texture, and structure throughout the year.

For example, in Vietnam, where Robusta is the predominant coffee variety, the calendar can be divided into four main stages:

  • Flower-bud initiation (November–December): The new season begins as coffee plants prepare to flower. Buds start to form and develop.

  • Flowering (January–March): White flowers appear, often after the first rains. This period is short but important: it signals the start of the productive phase.

  • Fruit setting and development (April–August): The flowers turn into small green berries, which grow in size over the months. The canopy stays dense and green.

  • Ripening and harvest (September–November): Berries ripen, changing color from green to red or yellow, depending on the variety. This is when harvesting takes place.
